# Prospecting Playbook — Product Identity

This playbook exists to prevent generic mass outreach. We contact brands because we observed a plausible unit-identity problem, not because they happen to use Shopify.

## Qualification score

Score each brand 0–2 on each signal:

- Own-brand durable physical product.
- Product value high enough for warranty/authenticity to matter.
- Warranty or product-registration page exists.
- Serial / IMEI / lot / unique-unit language is visible.
- Shopify storefront is active.
- Also sells via Amazon, retail, distributors or other channels.
- Public signs of manual registration/SAV workflow.
- Counterfeit/authenticity risk is plausible.
- Operational scale suggests >100 units/month.
- Relevant decision maker/contact route is public.

Prioritize brands scoring **12+ / 20**.

## Minimum prospect record

- company and website;
- vertical;
- Shopify evidence;
- Amazon/retail/distributor evidence;
- product price band;
- warranty/registration URL if public;
- serialization/authenticity signal;
- target role;
- public business contact route;
- qualification score;
- outreach status;
- observed problem hypothesis;
- notes and objections.

## Contact roles

For smaller brands: Founder / CEO / COO.

For larger brands: Head of Operations, Ecommerce Manager, Customer Experience/Warranty lead, After-sales Operations, Product/Hardware Operations.

Do not default to generic `contact@` when a relevant public role is available.

## Four-touch sequence

### Touch 1 — workflow question

Reference one observed fact and ask how the workflow is handled today. Do not lead with a feature list.

Example angle: a brand has a warranty page and sells on Shopify plus Amazon. Ask how individual product serials and warranty activation are reconciled across those channels.

### Touch 2 — focused follow-up

One sentence. Restate the operational problem, not the product.

### Touch 3 — visual proof

Send a 30–60 second demo/mock showing:

**scan QR → verify product → register ownership/warranty → merchant sees the unit.**

### Touch 4 — design-partner invite

Offer a constrained pilot with real units. No custom ERP promise.

## Discovery discipline

Do not ask “would you use this?”. Ask about:
- current process;
- volume;
- exceptions;
- time spent;
- failures;
- cost/risk;
- tools already tried;
- who owns the workflow;
- what happens across Shopify/Amazon/offline sales.

A problem repeated independently by five qualified brands is stronger evidence than 100 polite “sounds cool” replies.

## Prospect funnel

Initial test:

**60 qualified brands → 30 contacted → 15 substantive conversations → 5 workflow interviews → 2 design partners.**

This is a validation target, not a guaranteed conversion benchmark.

## Paid acquisition

Paid media is gated behind message-market evidence.

### First test
Budget ceiling: **$100–$150 total**.

Primary channel: Google Search, because queries can express active intent.

Initial themes:
- Shopify product registration app;
- Shopify warranty registration;
- serial number tracking Shopify;
- product authenticity QR;
- product warranty software for Shopify.

### Later channels
Meta and LinkedIn are primarily retargeting or narrow audience tests after organic messaging converts.

### Stop rules
Stop rather than “optimize harder” when:
- clicks are mostly consumers rather than brands;
- no qualified lead emerges from a meaningful sample;
- projected CAC is incompatible with $19–$99 monthly pricing;
- landing traffic does not understand the unit-level problem.

## Outreach ethics

Use public business information or permissioned data. Respect platform terms and rate limits. Do not buy dubious personal datasets, evade access controls or automate spam.

## Learning loop

Every repeated objection or failure should update one of:
- ICP;
- positioning;
- landing copy;
- pricing;
- onboarding;
- product scope;
- kill criteria.

That is RAIDER's Retroactive principle applied to go-to-market.
